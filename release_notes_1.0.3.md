# v1.0.3

Boom and Bust: a realm's people no longer only ever increase. Population now
grows and declines with how well a settlement actually fares, and a settlement
that keeps losing people falls back down the ladder — so the map stays a
pyramid of villages, towns and a few cities instead of drifting toward cities
everywhere.

## People come, and people go

- **Population moves both ways.** On top of natural increase (births, while a
  node is fed and warm), people migrate: a settlement or village that clearly
  out-earns its region and its kingdom attracts settlers and grows toward its
  ceiling, while one that is clearly poorer than its neighbours sheds people
  and declines toward a hard-scrabble remnant.
- **Wealth decides the direction.** "Wealth" is the same prosperity score the
  game already tracks (goods, tax and supply, scaled against the realm's
  health) — compared to the region's and the kingdom's average. Thrive and
  you grow; lag and you shrink; match your neighbours and you just tick
  along.

## The ladder goes both ways now

- **A City that keeps losing people falls back to a Town, and a Town back to
  a Village** — keeping its people, stores and name, and its trade follows it
  down. The result is a realistic spread: many farming villages, some
  crafting towns, and cities only where a settlement genuinely thrives.
- **Rising is earned, not inevitable.** Because a marginal settlement never
  reaches the population a raise needs, only the settlements that out-earn
  their surroundings ever climb a rung — cities stay rare and valuable
  without any hard cap on them.
- **The prompts say so.** A village that won't grow is told to make it thrive
  (out-earn its neighbours), not just keep it fed.
