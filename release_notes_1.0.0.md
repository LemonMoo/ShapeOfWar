# v1.0.0

The Living Land: the world is shaped by climate, not coincidence.

## Biomes follow real geography

- **Rainfall now comes from four real drivers instead of a noise blob.**
  Latitude bands (wet equator, dry subtropical belt, moist temperate,
  dry polar fringe), coast proximity (coasts are wet, deep continental
  interiors dry out), mountain rain shadows, and riverbanks. Jungle grows
  at the equator, desert in a ridge's rain shadow, taiga in the boreal
  belt, tundra at the poles — each for a reason the map can point to,
  instead of one biome covering a whole stretch of land by coincidence of
  noise.
- **Rain shadow.** Air moving in the prevailing wind (the same latitude
  wind bands that drive the ocean currents) drops its moisture climbing a
  mountain's windward slope and arrives dry on the far side — so a range is
  wet on one flank and desert on the other, the way real ranges are.
- **Temperature now falls with altitude.** High ground reads cold, so a
  mountain grades up through forest and foothills into bare alpine tundra
  at the treeline, rather than appearing as a solid wall with nothing
  between it and the plains.

## The land changes gradually

- **Transitional biomes bridge the jumps.** Savannah sits between desert
  and jungle, steppe between desert and plains, highland foothills between
  plains and mountain — so walking from a coast to a peak crosses a
  sequence of biomes, not a hard edge.
- **Rivers and lakes make wet banks.** Standing water keeps its banks wet,
  so swamps and wetlands form where water sits, instead of only where the
  rainfall noise happened to spike.

## Under the hood

- All twelve biome names are unchanged, so existing saves keep working
  exactly as before; the new terrain appears on newly generated worlds.
- Worldgen still reproduces cell-for-cell from its seed.
