# v0.13.0 — The Deep Places

There is a second world under the mountains now, and people living in it.

Three phases of the underworld ship together in this release: how you move
through it, how anything eats down there, and who was already living in it
when you arrived.

## Going down

A gate in the hillside is the only way in or out, and it costs a full day's
marching to pass — descending is an event, not a step. Below it, distance is
expensive for the reason it really was: **haulage**. A worked gallery is
harder going than a swamp, the worst ground on the surface, because it is
single-file and there is no going around it. A cavern is open floor and rather
quicker. The result is that a hold's own halls are close together and the next
massif is a genuine expedition, without a single rule anywhere saying so.

## Not seeing in the dark

Darkness is its own fog, and it does not work like the one above ground.
Light travels along **open passage**, never through rock — so a gallery ten
feet away on the other side of a wall is unknown until somebody actually walks
there. Owning three quarters of the surface reveals none of it. Exploring the
underworld is a real undertaking, and a lantern carried to a door shows you
the mouth and not the hall behind it.

## An economy with no sun

Nothing grows without light, so the underground **converts** rather than
farms:

- **Mushroom beds** turn manure, guano and spent pit timber into food. They
  are bounded by what there is to compost — beds with nothing in them produce
  nothing, however large they are.
- **Stalls** keep beasts below, out of the weather, and their muck is what the
  beds run on.
- **Gate holdings** are terraces and high pasture on the mountainside above a
  hold's own doors, worked from below. They are the only ground a hold can
  farm — and they can be taken from above, which is how you starve one.
- **Guano and blind cave fish** are the floor: poor, reliable, and never
  enough on their own.
- And a cave is the best larder anybody had before refrigeration. Food keeps
  roughly three times as long below ground, which turns a hold's early-game
  food problem into its late-game export.

## Who lives there

**Dwarf holds** are one great hall in the deepest rock — furthest from any
door, which is what defensible actually means — with mining villages around
it. A hold is born with terraces, stalls, beds and a full larder: it starts
fat, and has to have solved the problem before the stores run out.

**Goblin warrens** are many small hovels crowding the gates. No hall, no
terraces, no stores. They live on what they can scrape and on what they can
carry off — and their raiding is driven by their own hunger and by nothing
else. A warren beside a rich valley is a permanent nuisance; one beside a poor
valley is quiet, because there is nothing worth taking. Feed them, or garrison
the gate.

Your armies can march down and take the halls, and the AI understands a door
now: claiming underground is claiming through a chokepoint.

## Also in this release

- The alerts panel is drawn on parchment, with a wax seal on every warning.
- Gates that opened onto solid rock — one in three on some maps — are gone.
- A handful of fixes found by running three hundred real days with a hold in
  the world: beasts that could not be kept, terraces that grew no fodder, a
  warren with literally nothing to eat, and a larder that was being thrown
  away at world generation.

Saves from v0.12.0 load and play normally. An existing world has no underworld
under it — the mountains were carved before you got there, so a world
generated before this release stays the world it was.
