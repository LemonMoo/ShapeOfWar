## v0.4.0 — The Cartographer Update

A big one. The economy underneath the game has been rebuilt, and the Cartographer's Guild finally does the thing its name promises.

### Send someone to go and look

- **NEW: commission a survey.** A Guild will outfit a party, and it genuinely walks out into the black — charting a corridor of real country as it goes, aiming for the far edge of what its supplies will reach rather than the nearest scrap of unknown next door.
- Parties setting out from a port travel further and faster along the coast, further again with a shipyard. Coastlines map before interiors, the way they always did.
- A party can also fail to come home. Roughly one in six on a long inland expedition; less along the coast, because a faster journey is a shorter time exposed.

### Production is a decision now

- **NEW: villages have a finite workforce.** What a place produces is no longer a readout of the terrain under it — it is a question of where its hands go. Good land it hasn't the people to work stays unworked.
- **NEW: labour orders.** Tell a village what to concentrate on, and give that order to one village, a whole region, or the entire realm at once.
- Seasons fall out of this on their own: crops only harvest in season, so hands drift to the woods over winter without any rule saying so.

### Coin, and a reason to build

- **NEW: a real build menu** — a card for every building, ordered by what that place actually needs and why, instead of a stack of buttons that never said which mattered.
- **NEW: the Gold Mine and the Mint.** Gold is something you find, dig, haul and strike now, rather than a pile you start with and slowly spend.
- **CHANGED: buildings cost upkeep.** Every tier you put up wants timber every turn. Building is a commitment, not a one-off purchase.
- **CHANGED: timber is consumed.** Roofs rot and get rethatched. The woodpile finally has somewhere to go.
- **NEW: stockpile targets.** Say how much of a good a place should hold back before the rest is traded away.

### Expansion belongs to everyone

- **CHANGED: claiming wildland costs settlers and provisions**, not gold, timber and stone. You send people and you feed them until the first harvest — which is what taking new land actually cost.
- This fixes a real dead end: measured on a live save, **five of fourteen realms could not afford to expand at all**, most of them short of stone they had no mountains to quarry. Now none are locked out.
- Settlers come off the workforce back home, so growing genuinely competes with producing.

### Also

- **FIXED: the trade log never appeared when you clicked its tab.**
- Fresh fish is food. A fishing village can eat its own catch instead of watching it rot waiting for a smokehouse it hasn't got.
- Production no longer stops dead at a full store — it slows to a trickle instead of switching off.

---

The numbers in all of this are a first pass and want playing with rather than proving on paper. Expansion costs, survey prices and risk, and timber upkeep are the ones most likely to want a nudge.
