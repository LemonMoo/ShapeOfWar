# v0.12.0 — The Sound of Steel

The game owned eighty sound effects and played about a dozen of them. A battle
was one sword being drawn and then silence, while blades, shields, stone, fire
and death sat unused in the folder.

## A battle you can hear

Swords ring off shields, bows loose, bombs go off, a cavalry charge lands like
a charge, and a commander falling is audible over the rest of it.

That needs a rate limit or it is not a sound but a wall — a fight resolves
hundreds of blows a second. Every event the simulation drives has a minimum gap
between plays; every event **you** cause has none, because feedback that gets
dropped is worse than no feedback at all.

## 61 new sounds

Ten real sword attacks and ten real clashes, recorded as such. Marching feet,
rubble, gates, hammers, breaking timber, thunder. Goblin voices, and the roar
of whatever it is that holds the wildland.

Winning and losing a battle now sound like winning and losing a battle.

## Below the mountains

Go down and you hear the cave. The map theme gives way to it while you are
there, and comes back when you climb out. It is most of what makes descending
feel like going somewhere rather than switching a view.

## Credits

There is a Credits screen on the main menu now, naming everyone whose work this
game is built on.

That is not only manners. CC0 asks for nothing; CC-BY asks for a visible
credit — and until there was somewhere to put one, the game could only ever use
the first kind, which ruled out most of what exists. The screen is what makes
the rest usable, and the first attribution-licensed work in the game (Little
Robot Sound Factory's fantasy library, which supplies the goblins, the roar and
the cave) is on it, credited exactly as its author asks.
