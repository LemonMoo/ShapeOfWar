# v1.0.2

Governance & Development: a realm's growth is now a ladder of ages, and the
reason you can — or cannot — expand is a real one.

## Every realm has an age

- **Six ages — Homestead, Age of Villages, Age of Towns, Age of Cities,
  Age of Kingdoms, Age of Empire** — each reached by a concrete milestone
  on the settlement ladder, not by an invisible number. Raise a village to
  a Town, a Town to a City, raise a Castle, arm your levy, hold land and
  stock craft goods: each rung is a named achievement, and the region panel
  shows your age and exactly what builds the next one. Late game is a long
  road with visible milestones, not a sudden cliff.

## Expansion is governed, not gated

- **The flat "fill 50% of your villages" gate is gone.** A claim is always
  legal if you can pay it. What changes is the price: every settlement is a
  seat of government — a City governs 3 regions, a Castle 2, a Town 1, plus
  your capital's foothold. Hold more land than your government can run, and
  each further claim costs more settlers and provisions and runs slower.
- **This is a tax, not a wall.** Over-expansion is self-correcting: a realm
  that sprawls without building institutions pays more and more for each new
  region, while the answer to "why is this so dear" is always legible —
  build Towns, raise them to Cities, and raise Castles to govern more land.

## A reason to expand, and a reason not to

- **Crowding is the call.** When your own villages are nearly full, the
  claim panel tells you your people need land — the positive reason to reach
  for the frontier.
- **Absence is the answer.** A realm with room at home and full larders has
  no call to expand, and the panel no longer nags it to.
