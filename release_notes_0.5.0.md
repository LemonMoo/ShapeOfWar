## v0.5.0 — The Homelands Update

Where your people come from, what their country is called, and what they are good at.

### The world has kinds of country now

- **12 biomes instead of 6**, from a real temperature × moisture matrix rather than a threshold cascade that never saw temperature at all. The same rainfall used to produce the same terrain at the equator and at the pole. Latitude means something you can see and plan around.
- New: **highland, taiga, jungle, steppe, savannah, tundra.** Highland matters most — foothills that are ore-bearing but still farmable, which is what makes a mountain range habitable instead of a wall.
- **No biome is good at everything.** Plains are a breadbasket with no materials; highland is ore country with thin food; forest is timber and no grain. Terrain is asymmetric in *kind*, not in quality, so every realm needs something its neighbours have.

### Your people start where they come from

- **NEW: species homelands.** Elves open in forest, Dwarves in mountain and highland, Orcs on the savannah, Goblins in jungle and swamp. Humans are broad — their whole trait is being adaptable.
- Nothing about species touched placement before this. An Elf realm was as likely to begin in a desert as a Human one.
- It is a strong preference, not a guarantee. A map with no forest on it still places everyone; a species whose homeland is missing simply gets its next-best country.

### And they know it better than anyone else

- **NEW: native-terrain aptitude.** A Dwarf village in the highlands gets more out of the same ground than an Orc one would, and less out of a jungle.
- **NEW: acclimatisation.** Alien ground stops fighting you as your realm actually lives there — so expanding into unlike country is a choice rather than a trap. Taking someone's fields does not hand you their generations of learning them, though.

### Every region is a place

- **NEW: named country.** The Everwood, the Ashwaste, the Mistfen, the Skyteeth. The same forest reads as the Hollowpine in the frozen north and the Everwood on a warm coast.
- **NEW: Humans have a naming voice of their own** — all storms, oaths and stone. Realms like the Leeward Reach and Highstone Oathhold, cities like Stormspire and Thalseat, rulers like Queen Dalneth Stormcalled. They were the generic-medieval default before, which left the one species with no homeland of its own also the one with no voice of its own.

### Ore you can actually reach

- **NEW: the Mining Camp.** Villages sit on farmland and mountain is a twentieth of the map, so the entire mining tier was near-zero and Tools, Weapons and Shields effectively could not be made. A village can now work a seam nobody lives near.

### Fixes

- **Prosperity was a meter that could not move.** Every settlement in the game sat pinned near zero, for a reason that turned out to be arithmetic.
- **Nothing manufactured existed anywhere.** The domestic supply chain was severed in four separate places, and not one of them was a shortage.
- The trade log never appeared when you clicked its tab.
- Folding cards that would not fold, and a keyboard shortcut that only worked sometimes.

---

As always, the numbers are a first pass and want playing with rather than proving on paper. Homeland strength, how fast conquered land settles in, and the Mining Camp's reach are the ones most likely to want a nudge.
